Design objectives

Rainy Days explores a different design challenge from Legends of the Abyss: making a game immediately approachable without sacrificing meaningful decisions. It takes inspiration from War, a familiar card game centered on comparing cards, and aims to preserve that accessibility while introducing strategy and direct interaction between players. The intended audience is families and casual players who want a game they can learn quickly, finish in a short sitting, and immediately want to play again.

The design challenge

Easy to pick up

Keep the rules approachable for families and new players.

Brisk pacing

Favor tight gameplay and avoid unnecessary complexity.

Player interaction

Make the choices of other players matter to the experience.

Replayability

Give players reasons to return without relying on a long learning curve.

My contributions

I co-design the game with my business partner, developing and refining its mechanics and rules through prototyping and playtesting. My focus is on preserving the game’s accessibility while making room for strategic choices. Rather than introducing complexity for its own sake, we aim to make a small number of rules produce engaging interactions between players.

The design questions I focus on are whether a decision is worth the time it takes to make, whether players remain engaged with one another throughout the game, and whether a mechanic adds enough depth to justify the effort of learning it.

What this project demonstrates

Not every design problem benefits from adding more systems. Rainy Days is an exercise in designing around a small, tightly focused experience, where clarity, pacing, and social interaction are central goals. Its measure of success is not the number of mechanics it contains, but whether those mechanics create engaging choices without getting in the way of a quick, accessible game.
