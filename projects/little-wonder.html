Designing encounters

I designed 17 primary arenas, a hard-mode variant for each, the spaces between arenas, and the tutorial. The arena structure gave us a way to organize combat into discrete encounters and build levels around the revised gameplay mechanics.

The mid-development pivot

After receiving feedback, our team made a major change to the game’s core design. We moved toward arena-based combat, removed earlier mechanics, and introduced a new attack. Previous level work had to be discarded and rebuilt to support the new direction.

As both the software and level design lead, I was responsible for translating that design decision into a functioning game: rebuilding the encounter content while restructuring the gameplay architecture to support the change.

Connecting design and implementation

  • Designed the primary arena content, hard-mode variants, transitions, and tutorial.
  • Owned the core gameplay scene, integrating input, physics, rendering, and gameplay systems.
  • Implemented combat and collision systems and coordinated software integration across the team.

Takeaway

This project reinforced the value of being willing to discard work when the underlying design changes. Technical architecture and level content need to support the intended player experience, not constrain it simply because they already exist.
